Dr DeblinaBhattacharjee

Tenured Assistant Professor of Computer Science at the University of Bath, and Fellow of the Higher Education Academy.

I teach machines to understand, restore and reason about visual culture, from comics and film archives to sign language.

My group works on generative and multimodal models, 3D reconstruction, depth, saliency and multitask learning. We apply them to the visual arts and archives, where pictures carry culture; to British Sign Language, where they carry access for Deaf communities; and to medical imaging. I care about AI that is responsible and inclusive, and about how it meets human cognition.

Before Bath I did my PhD and a postdoc at the Image and Visual Representation Lab, EPFL, and worked at the Samsung–POSTECH Intelligent Media Research Centre and Google. I am Chief AI Advisor to Silence Speaks and lead the AI for Visual Arts workshop series.

Research

Four strands, one question: how should machines look at the things people make?

Visual arts and cultural heritage

Comics, manga, paintings and film archives break the assumptions of models trained on photographs. We build datasets and methods for depth, segmentation, saliency, retargeting and sketch-to-image generation in stylised art, and generative restoration for archives. I lead the AI for Visual Arts (AI4VA) workshop series at ECCV, ICCV and CVPR.

Accessible AI for Deaf communities

With Silence Speaks we are prototyping real-time, photorealistic 3D animatable British Sign Language avatars and a 3D dataset of 5,000 BSL signs, evaluated with Deaf end-users. Methods from our earlier work power the company’s avatar pipeline in use at Leeds Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust and with Cambridgeshire County Council.

Innovate UK Accelerated Knowledge Transfer, industry co-funded PhD studentship

Generative and multimodal learning

Editing-first 3D face models whose shape bases are learned per facial region, transformers that share attention across dense tasks, adapters that transfer to new tasks without fine-tuning, instance-aware image translation, and diffusion pipelines that ground language in the right region.

AI for healthcare

Physics-informed latent graphs that turn cardiac vortices into interacting nodes, with graph entropy as a marker of disease severity, developed with Dr Katharine Fraser’s group in Mechanical Engineering. Related work asks why retinal graders trained on colour fundus images fail on near-infrared ones.
